# Source look and feel: Rock Star Ate My Hamster (Codemasters, 1988/89)

Written from viewing Amiga screenshots (640x400) on 14 Oct 2026 (the C64 sites sit behind a bot check; the Amiga/ST/Spectrum versions share the same screen design). Reference only: nothing from the game is copied into the repo; this is an analysis of the "grammar" we want to echo, rendered much more richly.

## The grammar (what makes it feel like RSAMH)
1. **One fixed tableau per place.** Every location is a single cartoon "stage set" seen head-on, wide and shallow, in the middle band of the screen. The two main characters (the boss Cecil and sidekick Clive) are in nearly every shot: sometimes seen from BEHIND in their chairs looking at the thing (the telly, the mixing desk), sometimes sitting at the desk facing us. The room has comic clutter: posters of caricature faces on the wall, phone, ashtray with smoke, coffee cup, cigarette, a TV and video, blinds.
2. **Top: a big rounded speech-banner** across the whole screen (peach/tan gradient with an ordered-dither shade, thick dark outline, a pointed tail toward whoever is talking). All narration, jokes and headlines are delivered as the sidekick's chatter in small ALL-CAPS pixel type. The comedy is mostly TEXT, framed by this banner.
3. **Bottom: a permanent HUD strip**, always the same: a row of four gold-disc icons on the left (the goals: here gold records, for us the four Gold Number Plates), a green CASH bar with the money figure under them, a cluster of portrait faces in the centre (the current band: for us the current/selected car), a torn-off wall-calendar sheet (MON 01 JAN, big day number) and a white "SALES" chart panel on the right (for us net worth / bookings chart).
4. **In-scene pop-up menu**, not full-screen menus: a small boxed list (FORWARD / REWIND / SELECT) with an arrow cursor beside the TV. Choosing something advances the calendar a day.
5. **Faces are the art.** Big, expressive, grotesque-comic caricatures with heavy black outlines; skin in peach with ordered-dither shading; strong flat room colours (pink walls, blue room, wood panelling), a limited 16-32 colour palette, hard pixels, dither for gradients, saturated 80s palette. Static scenes (few screens) but every one is full of jokes and period detail.
6. **Parody everywhere**: invented famous names, posters, charts, newspaper headlines; the visual gag is in the props.
7. **Structure**: menu-driven management, point-and-select, one action per day, the day ticks on the calendar.

## How we echo it (our version)
- Keep the grammar: fixed tableau per place (Mark at his desk facing us, Dave beside him, or both seen from behind facing the Webb TV); top speech-banner with Dave's chatter and the headlines; the persistent bottom HUD strip (4 Gold Number Plate slots, CASH bar, selected-car portrait, torn-off calendar day, small chart panel); small in-scene pop-up menus with an arrow cursor.
- Modernise the rendering, not the layout: **1280x720 HD pixel art** (pixel size about 2-4 px, many more colours per material ramp than 1989, soft dither only where it adds texture), textured materials (wood grain, wallpaper, paper, enamel signs, chrome), warm lamp light vs cold window light, baked soft shadows and ambient occlusion, subtle bloom and film grain, dust and steam, parallax on window and background layers, animated details (kettle steam, TV static, cigarette smoke becomes tea steam, flickering strip light), chunky pixel type (Press Start 2P / VT323 style), and juicy transitions.
- The cars are the "band": the classic cars are our big caricature faces: gorgeous, glossy, characterful HD pixel sprites shown on the telly, in the garage and on the HUD portrait.
- British, affectionate, silly. Text is the comedy; the picture frames it.
